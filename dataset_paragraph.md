## Paragraph (paste as-is, then add group member names at the top of the proposal)

> The data for this project come from the King County Metro General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS) schedule feed, published by King County Metro Transit and available at https://metro.kingcounty.gov/GTFS/google_transit.zip (mirror: https://www.soundtransit.org/GTFS-KCM/google_transit.zip). I downloaded the feed on November 18, 2026; it is version FAL26-161.5, covering service from October 29, 2026 to March 26, 2027. The feed covers Metro Transit bus, Seattle Streetcar, King County Water Taxi, and Sound Transit Link light rail and some Sound Transit Express service (agencies in the feed are Metro Transit, City of Seattle, Sound Transit, and Solid Ground EZ Loop), with 142 routes and 31,484 trips. Because GTFS is published schedule data, the network represents scheduled service, not observed ridership or real-time operations. I will build a one-mode, undirected, unweighted network in Python (networkx and pandas) in which each node is a transit stop and each tie connects two stops that appear consecutively on the same trip in stop_times.txt. Built this way, the network has 6,271 nodes (stops) and 7,025 ties (unique consecutive-stop pairs). It has 4 components, with the largest component containing 6,238 stops (99.47% of all stops), which gives a natural baseline for the largest-component resilience measure. King County requires the credit line "Transit scheduling, geographic, and real-time data provided by permission of King County" on anything displaying the data, and I will include it on all figures. One limitation to report transparently is that these counts use raw stop_ids: the same physical stop is often split into multiple stop_ids by direction or bay, and bus–Link transfers are not ties unless those stops are merged, so I will apply a stated merge rule (parent_station, or stop name plus rounded coordinates) and report how the node and tie counts change.

- Framing note (do not paste into the dataset paragraph, but use in the Question section): do not oversell the network as strongly scale-free by degree — there are no large degree hubs. The resilience hypothesis is better stated as robust to random failure but fragile to betweenness-targeted removal (bridges/chokepoints such as the I-90 corridor and the Link spine).
